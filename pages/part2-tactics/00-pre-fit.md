## 0 - Pre-fit

If you can make it through agency-land, you've got a pretty good chance that you can make it as a SaaS founder.

Nathan Latka, an ex-SaaS founder and now host of the biggest SaaS podcasts deemed the "agency-to-SaaS" hack.

Through his 3,000 interviews with SaaS founders, he says for these reasons they're the best suited:

* Agency founders have **validated market insights** from direct client work (real needs > hypothetical)
* They possess **financial runway** from service revenue to fund product development
* **Built-in customer validation** through existing client relationships

"agency owners in their 20's make the best SaaS CEOs in their 30's."

David Hart, the "agency-to-SaaS" guy himself, also said: If I were a betting man, I'd bet on the former founders of an agency before I'd bet on a couple of bright guys fresh out of McKinseys.

For all these reasons:

1. Domain Experience
2. Time, Money, and a Ready-made team
3. Absence of Desperation
4. Network
5. Reputation
6. Battle Scars
7. You're like a Swiss Army Knife
8. Great Sales Person
9. IP Balance
10. First Customers Already Built in
11. Capital Efficiency

Based on over 10 Agency-to-SaaS transformations along with Nathan's framework and David's book, here is a quick rubric to evaluate your agency:

* Clients
  * validated inefficiencies / problems (1 of 3 from Nathan's hack)
  * connections
  * access to initial user-base (2 of 3 from Nathan's hack)
* Resources (profit, not revenue)
  * runway: cost to build, cost to market & sell.
  * A moat is defined as putting in resources (capital, time, expertise)
* Longer lifetime
  * More domain expertise
  * More experience (has lived to see more problems)

David created this Matrix (insert Matrix): Product Experience and Sector Experience.

As an agency owner, you're going to be on the very right side, with varying Product Experience. All you're missing is someone with experience building products, like an engineer - unless you already have that and you're perfect.
